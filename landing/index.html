§ 01 — How it works

Three steps, zero automation

Nothing runs in the background and nothing sends on its own — it's your list, your words, your tap.

01

Import

Add your list — drop in a spreadsheet or paste in profile links. Adding the same list again just fills in anything new, it won't create duplicates.

02

Personalize

We write a first draft for every person on your list, using their name and what they post about — and we vary the wording each time so no two messages sound copy-pasted.

03

Send

One tap opens the message right in their inbox, already typed out. You just look it over and hit send — nothing goes out on its own.

§ 06 — FAQ

Questions people actually ask

Does this violate X's rules?

Every DM is a real tap from your own logged-in session — the app never touches the X API and never sends anything on its own. It prefills the compose box and warns you as you approach whatever daily cap you set; you still decide, and tap, for every message.

What if a lead doesn't have a numeric id?

The Needs ID filter finds those leads. The send button falls back to copying the message and opening the profile — one paste and you're sending the same way. Most scraper exports (Apify, PhantomBuster, TweetScout) already include the id.

Where does my data live?

Right in your browser by default — nothing leaves your device. Turn on optional account sync and it's also backed up securely in the cloud, visible only to you, so it follows you to your other devices.

Can I use it on my phone?

Yes — open the app in Safari, Share → Add to Home Screen, and it installs as a standalone app that works offline. DMs still open the X app to send.

What lists actually work?

A CSV export from Apify, PhantomBuster, or TweetScout; a bare column of profile links; or rows pasted straight into the import box. Columns auto-match by header, and you can fix any mapping before importing.

Is there an API key or bot account involved?

No. No API keys, no bot account, no headless browser session. The only thing DM Aura automates is the typing — you still do the sending.
